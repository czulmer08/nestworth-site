# NestWorth Financial Model Specification

**Purpose.** This is the single source of truth for what every financial term in NestWorth *means* mathematically. The Google Sheet is the durable record; the deterministic JavaScript in `app.html` is the calculation engine; the UI and Wren only *interpret* these numbers. Keep this file in sync with the code so a change six versions from now can't quietly redefine "Available" or "Cash flow."

Applies to: **app.html v0.37.0** (build 20260828.53) and forward. When a formula here and the code disagree, that's a bug — fix one to match the other and note it in the changelog.

---

## 0. The three concepts, kept separate

NestWorth deliberately answers three *different* questions. Never conflate them; label each one.

| Concept | Question it answers | Time frame |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual plan** | Does the whole year balance? | Full calendar year |
| **Cash flow** | Does the timing work month to month? | Month-by-month running balance |
| **Net worth / position** | What do we own minus owe, right now? | A point-in-time snapshot |

A household can pass the **annual plan** and still fail **cash flow** (a $4,000 insurance bill lands in February before the money accumulates), and both are separate from **net worth**.

## 0a. Where each surface lives (the tab structure, v0.68.31+)

The app reuses the six tabs it has, each mapped to what it answers — **Add · Month · Budget · Goals · Worth · Settings** — rather than adding a dedicated "answers" tab. (v0.68.29 briefly split Budget into Plan+Outlook as a 7th tab; v0.68.31 retired that in favor of the mapping below.)

- **Add** — log an expense or deposit (home base). Deposit progress shows income vs the plan (§0b).
- **Month** — the **current outlook**: it leads with the one answer that matters — *how much is safe to move to your goal today* (the §7b goal-safe panel, `#mGoalSafe`) — then the *Contingency & envelopes* buffer status (§8), then the retrospective "where the money went" (this-month total, by category vs budget, reminders, last-month review, insights).
- **Budget** (`view-budget`) — the **plan you set**, with the annual verdict on top: *Does this budget work?* (§5, feasibility + Run-a-what-if, §12) sits at the top, then Categories (with the per-category leftover rule: Stays here / Contingency buffer), Expected income, Quick setup, the year picker.
- **Worth** (`view-worth`) — the **forecast / big picture**: net worth over time (§10) and *Cash flow through the year* (§6, the month-by-month projected-cash grid, Nest Egg Floor and Starting-savings inputs, `#stGrid`/`#stVerdict`). (The annual *Does this budget work?* verdict lives on Budget, v0.68.33+.)
- **Goals / Settings** — unchanged.

Mapping rule of thumb: **Budget = what I intend (inputs); Month = am I okay right now / what can I safely do; Worth = how does the year/future look.** Card IDs, render functions, and handlers are unchanged across this reorganization — it is pure information architecture, the engines are untouched. `switchView` keeps `"plan"`→Budget and `"outlook"`→Month as back-compat aliases. Wren's "Show me" follows the mapping — setup questions open Budget, buffer/this-month/safe-to-move open Month, cash-flow/afford/decision/feasibility open Worth. Invariants: `verify-tab-layout.js`.

## 0b. Three money questions the UI must never conflate

Separate from the §0 triad, three *quantities* look similar and are routinely confused. NestWorth keeps them in distinct language and distinct surfaces; they may be related in a given month but are never interchangeable.

| Quantity | What it means | Where it's shown | Wording rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Budget remaining** | Spending-budget capacity left this month = `Σ mbud − budUsed − goalHold − unbudgeted` (§7). Not cash on hand. | Add-screen top card | "$X left **in this month's budget**" — never bare "left this month" (reads as cash). |
| **Income above plan** | Actual deposits − planned deposits for a source = `mdep − mtarget`. Only means more income arrived than the plan expected. | Add-screen deposit progress | "+$X **above plan** ✓" / "$Y **still expected**". NEVER "surplus / free / available / left over" — those imply it's spendable, which this number does not establish. |
| **Safe to move** | The largest amount movable to a goal today with every future month ≥ the Nest Egg Floor = `max(0, forwardLow − floor)` (§7b). | Month (goal-safe panel, `#mGoalSafe`) | The only number that answers "can I fund the goal." Derived from the forward projection, never from the two above. |

Being **above plan on income does not mean that money is available** — above-plan dollars may be needed for above-plan/unbudgeted spending, envelope repair, future bills, or the floor. Only the forward projection (§7b) decides what portion is truly free, and it does so without double-counting (each future commitment is already in the projected path). Invariants: `verify-deposit-progress.js` (Add wording), `verify-goal-safe.js` (safe-to-move).

## 0c. "Your money right now" — the whole-cash reconciliation (`moneyRightNow`, Month tab)

The Month tab reconciles the **total available-cash** position into what is safe to move, answering *"where is my money committed, and what is safe to move?"* The primary panel is a **provable identity** read straight from the forward projection (`goalSafeToMove`, §7b), never a set of independent subtractions:

> **available === reserve + floor + safe**
> where `available` = the current-month projected cash (`goalSafeToMove.currentProjected`), `reserve` = `available − forwardLow` (what must stay to ride out upcoming net outflows until the tightest month), `floor` = the Nest Egg Floor, and `safe` = `goalSafeToMove.safeToGoal` (= `forwardLow − floor`). Algebraically `reserve + floor + safe = (available − forwardLow) + floor + (forwardLow − floor) = available`, so the three parts always sum exactly (non-breach).

**Why it cannot double-count.** `computeCashflow` already embeds upcoming bills, envelope set-asides and goal holds in the path, so they are already *inside* `reserve`. The panel therefore names them within the reserve ("your envelopes and goal set-asides are already counted inside that reserve") and never subtracts them again as separate lines — this is the deliberate reason the panel does **not** render the tempting five-way "bills / envelopes / contingency / floor / goals" split, which would double-count obligations already in the projection. Contingency repair is likewise not a cash line here: it is rebuilt by future underspending, not by holding cash back (§8, "coverage never touches cash flow").

**One card, four layers (v0.68.34).** This is the single "Your money right now" card on Month — the safe-to-move panel and the former standalone "Contingency & envelopes" card are consolidated so the screen never shows competing "money remaining" numbers. (1) **Answer** — a single figure, `Safe to move to [goal]`, shown as the *conclusion of the waterfall*. The label is count-aware (`goalMoveName()`): the safe figure is goal-AGNOSTIC (total cash movable to any savings/goal), so it names the goal only when exactly one is active, says "your goals" with several (plus a split note), and "savings" with none (no floating hero on top; the number appears once). (2) **Why** — the floor-independent waterfall above it (`available → reserved → floor → safe`); its independence is proven, so the three are genuine separate deductions, and the **Reserved for what's ahead** row is itself an inline disclosure — tapping it opens a month-by-month reconciliation of the reserve (`reserveBreakdown()`): for each future month up to the tightest, the amount that drives projected cash to a *new low below today* — non-negative figures that sum **exactly** to the reserve — with that month's planned expenses & commitments and income arriving; the projection methodology sits behind a nested "How is this calculated?". Hierarchy: *what's ahead → why today's cash must cover it → how it's computed*. (3) **This month** — deposits received (`rc.income`), actual spending (`rc.consumption`), and *Cash not yet spent* (`income − consumption`); the term "surplus" is deliberately not used. (4) **Your protection** — contingency available (`max(0, rc.contingencyEffective)`) and banked in envelopes (`computeRollover().envTotal`), each with a one-line descriptor; (4b) **Your goals** — goal funding supportable (`goalFundingStatus().residualSupported`, $/mo), kept separate because it is savings capacity, not a reserve. **Not double-reserved:** contingency and envelopes are labels on cash already flowing through the projection, so the safe figure is identical whether a category is plain, an envelope, or a buffer (proven in `verify-money-now.js`); only the Nest Egg Floor is a real deduction. *Available cash now* and *Nest Egg Floor* are tappable for provenance/definition (the floor is a total-cash minimum, explicitly distinguished from the contingency cushion). Progressive disclosure holds the rest behind expandables — "what the reserve protects", **View contingency history** (the trend chart, §8), **View envelopes** (per-envelope balances), and "Where did the extra $X go?" (the `monthlyCashReconciliation` allocation, explanation-only, never the source of `safe`). Nothing is removed — the old "where this month's cash is going" waterfall (a duplicate of "where did the extra go") and the standalone card are retired; the reconciliation math remains in `monthlyCashReconciliation`/`verify-cash-allocation`. In breach, `safe` is $0 and the card states the gap instead of a positive number. Invariants: `verify-money-now.js`, `verify-contingency-trend.js`.

## 1. The ledger row

Every transaction is one row, columns A–I:

`[ A Year, B Month(1–12), C Date(YYYY-MM-DD), D Contributor, E Category, F Company, G Amount, H Description, I Reimbursed(Y/N) ]`

- **Expense row:** `Category ≠ "Deposit"` and Category/Company is not a goal name. `Amount > 0` reduces the category's remaining budget.
- **Credit / refund row:** an expense row with `Amount < 0`. It *lowers* spending in that category (money came in), it is **not** income.
- **Income (deposit) row:** `Category == "Deposit"`. `Amount > 0`.
- **Goal movement:** Category or Company equals a goal name — a savings earmark, **not** an expense and **not** income. Excluded from both.

**One engine, every screen.** The same ledger must produce the same number everywhere. Three shared functions are the only place monthly money is derived, and Month, Cash flow, Nest Review, Insights, the Decision Engine, and Wren all consume them: `monthActualTotals(y, mo[, {asOf}])` → `{income, expense}` (net expense over ALL rows — budgeted, uncategorized, unbudgeted — via the classifier below; excludes goal moves & future-dated rows; the optional `{asOf}` supplies an explicit day cutoff for a prior-month same-point comparison, defaulting to today's as-of). **Every surface that says "spent this month" — the Month headline, Nest Review, and Wren's month-over-month comparison — uses this one helper; Wren keeps no separate expense filter** (a v0.68.3 fix: the comparison branch had its own `expOf()` that dropped uncategorized spending and used the real clock). `goalContribMonth(name, y, mo)` → money moved into a goal that month (matched by name in **Category or Company**); and `actualIE(rows, y)` → the 12-month `{ai, ae, ag}` actual income / expense / goal-movement arrays the cash-flow and net-worth engines run on. `verify-golden.js` pins one synthetic household (income $5,000; $3,000 spending; a $200 refund; $400 unbudgeted; $850 into a goal via Category and Company; $10,000 checking, $2,000 debt, a $1,000 stand-alone goal) and asserts that **Month, Cash flow, Budget, Goals, Worth, Nest Review, the Decision Engine, and Wren** all read the same authoritative numbers ($5,000 income / $3,200 net spend / $850 goal / $950 spendable-cash net / $9,000 net worth) — the test that catches a surface keeping its own copy of a formula, and the one to extend before trusting any new screen.

**Property-based & mutation testing (the two strongest tiers).** `verify-fuzz.js` throws **9,000 randomized valid households** (mixed income types, fixed/residual/linked goals, envelopes, buffers, assets, debts) at the REAL engines and asserts IDENTITIES rather than examples: a +$X recurring expense can never improve cash flow (low or surplus); +$X income can never worsen it; a goal-monthly increase lowers spendable cash but never changes wealth (net-worth trajectory end); the Decision Engine's "before" exactly equals the live `planMetrics(currentPlan())`; moving money between tracked assets doesn't change net worth; changing an **account-earmarked** goal's balance doesn't change net worth (earmarking keys on `g.account`, NOT `g.category` — a category-linked goal with no account is stand-alone savings that DOES count); the annual-plan identity `surplus === income − budget − goalSave` holds in both modes; and every contingency figure reconciles (coverage ≤ available, ≤ deficit, per-envelope sums to `contingencyUsed`, `totalUncovered === uncoveredEnvelope + bufferDeficit`, nothing negative or created). `verify-property.js` adds the residual-allocation invariants (4,000 randomized households: never negative, never overdraw, exact reconciliation) and the `monthActualTotals` as-of unit tests. **`verify-mutation.js` is the test-of-the-tests**: it surgically sabotages the app (surplus sign flip, as-of removed, 3-paycheck check-count ignored, residual penny → last element, contingency coverage ignoring the buffer, budget-used doubled, net worth ignoring debts, uncategorized spending dropped, deposits miscounted as expense), runs a targeted suite against each mutant, and requires it to turn RED — a surviving mutant is a coverage gap. All 12 (the nine above plus FIN-ASOF-001/002/NW-001) are caught. **Validity gate (what makes the green trustworthy):** every mutation carries three-phase evidence — the target suite must PASS CLEANLY on the untouched app (baseline), then FAIL BY ASSERTION on the mutant (ran to its printed verdict with ≥1 failed check — not merely a nonzero exit), then PASS CLEANLY again after byte-exact restore. Anything that is not a clean pass or a genuine assertion failure — a missing dependency (e.g. Playwright not installed), a browser-launch failure, a syntax error, a timeout, or any crash before the suite prints its summary — is classified **INVALID (inconclusive), never "caught"**, so a broken environment can't green-wash a crash into a passing mutation. **It temporarily rewrites `app.html` (restoring it byte-exact in `finally`), so it must NEVER run in the parallel suite batch** — run it alone.

**Executable hardening harnesses (labeled for what they prove, and what they don't).** `verify-chaos.js` injects transport failures at the `fetch` layer so the REAL write paths (`api`/`claimRows`/`vappend`/`sweepStaleClaims`) run under adversity — proving bounded retry on idempotent 429/5xx, no retry of the non-idempotent append, reservation fail-closed on a lost verification read, partial-reservation release, stale-nonce sweep, ambiguous-write surfacing an error (never a silent success), and 401→session-expired. **It cannot prove live two-device simultaneous-write atomicity — that stays the documented client-only concurrency boundary.** `verify-perf.js` is a headless-Chromium regression BASELINE at 1k–100k rows (100k: index ~14ms, applyBudget ~97ms, Month render ~188ms, Wren ~48ms, ~27MB heap) — relative-only, not iPhone timings, not a "feels native" claim. `verify-security.js` is code-level: formula-injection sanitizing on write paths, live-XSS render checks for every user string, share-link-not-writer, starter-copy sanitized before any public grant, and the **honest token policy** — it drives the real `saveTok`/`storedToken`/`clearTok` and proves only the short-lived access token + expiry is cached (exact `{t,e}` shape), that a refresh/id token fed in is NEVER persisted, that an expired token is refused, and that sign-out clears it (it asserts the ACTUAL documented policy, not a false "tokens never persisted" claim) — not a substitute for an independent adversarial/live-OAuth audit. `verify-starter-share.js` drives the REAL `shareStarterCopy` under fetch fault-injection at every stage (copy, ledger delete, NW/history clear, meta strip, each verification read, and a *silent* clear that returns 200 but leaves data behind) and proves the anyone-with-link permission grant is NEVER issued unless sanitation AND read-after-write verification both succeeded, that the unsafe copy is deleted on any failure, and that the happy path shares exactly once, only after verification — the starter-copy flow now FAILS CLOSED. `verify-a11y.js` is a code-level accessibility audit (zoom enabled, dark mode, `lang`, `alt`, reduced-motion honoured; overlays now announced as modal dialogs — `openOv` sets `role="dialog"` + `aria-modal` and moves focus in — and every icon-only button carries an `aria-label`, both asserted as regression guards) — NOT a screen-reader/on-device certification. `verify-idempotent.js` drives the REAL `idempotentAppend`/`reconcilePendingTxns` against a simulated Sheets backend to prove the durable-transaction-ID layer: a normal append commits row + id and clears its marker; an ambiguous write (server commits, client throws) leaves the marker; reconcile clears the marker with NO re-append when the id is already in column J, and re-appends exactly once with the SAME id when it isn't — every id appears at most once. **It proves single-client reconciliation of an interrupted own write, still not live two-device atomicity.**

**Concurrency is best-effort, not atomic.** The nonce row-reservation greatly reduces same-instant clobbering between two household devices but cannot guarantee exclusivity (read→write→verify can interleave); a real guarantee needs a server-side lock (Apps Script). **Reservation FAILS CLOSED**: if the verification read fails, ownership is UNKNOWN — never "assume ours". `claimRow` returns 0 (caller retries the whole action rather than risk clobbering another device); `claimRows` releases every confirmed reservation from that attempt and returns null so `vappend` falls back to Google's append (which can't lose a write). Partial multi-row reservations are always released on any bail, so an interrupted claim can't strand nonce rows, and abandoned `~clm<ts>~…` markers are swept by age (`sweepStaleClaims`, on load). **Transient-error retry** is idempotency-scoped: reads and range-addressed writes (`values:batchUpdate`/`batchClear`) retry a 429/5xx or network blip with bounded exponential backoff + jitter (≤3); the non-idempotent `values:append` is NEVER blindly retried. The **ambiguous write** (Google commits an append, the network drops before the 200 arrives) is now handled by a **durable-transaction-ID idempotency layer**: `idempotentAppend(values9)` writes the row plus a durable id (`NW-<ts36><rand>`) in a technical **column J** — outside the styled A:I table, ignored by the data model that reads `A2:I` — and records a `localStorage` marker (`nw_txn_<id>`, scoped to `sheetId`) BEFORE the append. Because column J is written atomically with the row, the id lands with the row or not at all. On load, `reconcilePendingTxns()` reads `LEDGER!J2:J` and, for each pending marker for the current budget, decides by EXACT id: present ⇒ the write landed, clear the marker (no re-append); absent ⇒ re-append once with the SAME id (so a second interruption still can't duplicate). `ensureLedgerTxnCol()` best-effort widens the ledger grid to ≥10 columns; a read failure during reconcile leaves the marker for the next load rather than guessing. Wired into the deposit flow and into `loadAll` (after `reconcilePending`). Immediate same-session re-taps of an identical entry remain guarded by `hasDupDeposit`/`hasDupExpense`. Honest guarantee: single-device writes strong; an interrupted own write is now reconcilable-without-duplication across reloads; ordinary multi-device use reasonably protected; exactly-simultaneous conflicting writes from two live devices remain best-effort, not atomic. Multi-step writes that touch balances and the ledger (debt payoff) record their intent in `localStorage` first (`reconcilePending`); if the ledger half never lands, the next load completes it once (matched against the ledger so it never double-writes). Defense-in-depth, not a substitute for atomicity. **Engine invariants** `FIN-ASOF-001` (future expense excluded from actuals), `FIN-ASOF-002` (future goal move excluded from goal actuals), and `FIN-NW-001` (goal funding doesn't lower net worth) have dedicated tests (`verify-engine-invariants.js`) — the mutation audit proved the 64-test suite alone couldn't catch their removal; all three mutations now die.

**Canonical classification (one definition, every model).** Three functions are the single source of truth — `isDepositRow(r)` (`E == "deposit"`), `isGoalMovementRow(r)` (`isGoalName(E) || isGoalName(F)` — a goal earmark tagged by **either** Category or Company), and `isExpenseRow(r)` (`!isDepositRow && !isGoalMovementRow` — every other row, including an uncategorized outflow, which nets credits down). `actualIE(rows, y)` builds the actual income/expense 12-month arrays from these. Every forecast model — `computeCashflow`, `computeNetWorthTrajectory`, and the Budget screen's `renderFeas` — calls `actualIE`, so none of them can re-derive a slightly different rule. (Earlier the forecast models only checked Category, so a goal movement tagged by Company could leak into actual spend; that's fixed.)

## 2. Spent

- **Spent, category, month** = `Σ Amount` over expense rows for `(currentYear, currentMonth, thisCategory)`, folding itemized bills into their parent. In code: `catSpend12(name)[currentMonth-1]` (`state.spend` is the current-year 12-month index built by `buildIndexes()`).
- **Spent, category, year** = `Σ` of that category's 12 monthly values.
- **Spent, total, month/year** = `Σ Amount` over all expense rows in the period.

Credits (negative rows) net against spend, so "Spent" is spending **net of refunds**.

## 3. Budget

- **Category monthly budget** `mbud` = `bud12[currentMonth-1]` (a per-month array, so seasonal categories can vary).
- **Category annual budget** `annual` = `Σ bud12`.
- **Weekly income** is annualized as `weekly × 52 ÷ 12`; **biweekly** as `paycheck × 26 ÷ 12`. These are **planning averages**, not a real payday calendar — never call them an exact monthly cash forecast. (A real biweekly year has ten 2-paycheck months and two 3-paycheck months.)

**Paycheck-budgeting modes (three, all legitimate — the model must distinguish them, not collapse them).** A paycheck line names its full take-home per check plus how much of each check is actually budgetable. With `n` = the real number of paydays in a month (from `paydaysPerMonth` when "Use real paydays" is on; otherwise the annual total is `_spread` across 12), and **every result floored at $0**:
  - **Budget a % of each check** (`mode:"pct"`, `pct`): `available = paycheck × n × pct%`. Scales with `n`.
  - **Set aside $ per check** (`mode:"keepcheck"`, `keepChk`): `available = (paycheck − keepChk) × n`. The set-aside scales with `n` — a 3-paycheck month diverts more than a 2-paycheck month. This is the mode for "$1,769.60 of every check auto-goes elsewhere."
  - **Set aside $ per month** (`mode:"keep"`, `keep`): `available = paycheck × n − keep`. The set-aside is flat regardless of `n`. This is the mode for "reserve $3,169.89 this month, whether 2 or 3 checks arrive."
  These are **not** interchangeable: per-check and per-month give different monthly figures whenever `n ≠ 26/12`, which is why using per-month for a per-check reality under-budgets 3-paycheck months (the original Denzell bug).

  **Mid-year pay changes** (`cfg.raises` = `[{month 2-12, amount}]`, sorted ascending). The base `cfg.amount` is the take-home from January; from each change's month on, the new take-home applies. `monthsFor` computes each month at its **effective** take-home (`_effAmt(mi)` = the last change at/or before month `mi+1`, else base), so a raise in July lifts only Aug… and, under the real-payday calendar, the effective amount still multiplies by that month's real check count (a 3-check August in a $2,500 window = $7,500). With no calendar, checks distribute evenly (`checks/12` per month) at each month's effective rate. **A config with no `raises` takes the exact pre-feature path** (single `perCheckNet`, `_spread` annual) — byte-identical, so the whole existing suite still holds. Invariants: `verify-midyear-pay.js` (steps at Apr/Sep, calendar + flat, keepcheck + raise, chain, no-change unchanged).

  **The budget wizard preserves what it can't render.** The wizard's income step only exposes freq, take-home, and a %/set-aside/all keep mode — it has no control for a per-check set-aside (`keepcheck`), real paydays (`calendar`/`payAnchor`), mid-year `raises`, or non-paycheck income types (custom/itemized/split/onetime). So `openWizard` attaches the original cfg as `_cfg`, and `wizIncCfg` **carries those fields through** rather than rebuilding a lossy cfg from scratch: it keeps `keepcheck`+`keepChk` when the keep mode is left on its default, always carries `calendar`/`payAnchor`/`raises`, and returns the original richer income cfg untouched when the displayed monthly wasn't edited (an explicit edit still downgrades, as intended). Likewise `wizCatCfg` carries a monthly envelope's `rollStart` so a wizard re-run doesn't reset its rollover history. Invariants: `verify-wizard-preserve.js`. Rule going forward: **any income/category field the wizard has no UI for must be carried from `_cfg`, never dropped.** One materialization — `monthsFor(cfg)` — writes the 12-month `bud12`; **every** downstream surface (monthly plan `_cfArrays`, Month "expected income"/deposit-completeness `bud12[m-1]`, Cash Flow, Annual Plan, Decision Engine, Wren) reads that same `bud12` and may never re-derive income from the cfg. **Permanent invariants** (`verify-percheck-chain.js`, whole chain, all three modes): `$3,836.27` biweekly with `$1,769.60`/check → **2 checks $4,133.34 · 3 checks $6,200.01**; same with `$3,169.89`/month → **2 checks $4,502.65 · 3 checks $8,338.92**; 54% scales 1.5× from a 2- to a 3-check month; a set-aside larger than a month's pay floors at $0.

**Over budget (this month)** = a category (excluding envelope-rolling ones) where `mbud > 0` and `Spent > mbud`. Amount over = `Spent − mbud`.

**Exact-total rounding.** When an amount is spread across months (split, save-up, itemized `AnnualSaveUp`, annualized weekly/biweekly/paycheck income), each month is rounded to cents but the leftover penny goes into the **last participating month** (`_spread`), so the twelve values always re-sum to the exact annual total — `$100 ÷ 3 = 33.33 + 33.33 + 33.34`, never `99.99`.

**Save-up start month.** An itemized `AnnualSaveUp` bill spreads its yearly amount evenly from its **start month** (`it.start`, default January) through the due month. A bill set up mid-year should set the start to the current month so each remaining payment is right ($1,200 due December, started in August → $240 × 5, not $100 × 12 with the Jan–Jul contributions phantom-assumed).

## 4. Plan + Actual (the current-month rule)

For any month `m` in "actual" mode:

- `m` **before** the current month → use **actual** logged values.
- `m` **is** the current month → use **`max(actual-to-date, plan)`** for both income and expense, taken **independently** on each side. Rationale: if you've already blown the budget this month, that overage shows up *now*; if you're under, assume you'll spend up to plan. This is the "so far + plan" blend. The two maxes are independent and income is counted **once** (a max, never a sum) — so this does **not** double-count income and is **not** a catch-up artifact. One honest consequence: mid-month, if a large deposit has already landed (actual income > plan) but the month's spending hasn't been logged yet (actual outflow < plan), the current month pairs the **real** high income with the **planned** (higher-than-logged) outflow. That makes the current-month net the month's most *optimistic* reading — it only **shrinks** as real spending is logged (outflow rises toward, then past, plan), never inflates. Proven in `verify-cashflow-current-month.js`.
- `m` **after** the current month → use **plan**.

Code: `renderStress()` (cash-flow grid) and `renderFeas()` (annual feasibility). Both use this identical rule.

## 5. Annual plan feasibility

```
surplus_or_shortfall = annualIncome − annualCategoryBudget − annualFixedGoalSavings
monthly_average      = surplus_or_shortfall ÷ 12
```

- `annualFixedGoalSavings` = `Σ (goal.monthly × 12)` over goals that are **not** category-linked and **not** residual (those are already represented elsewhere — no double counting).
- In "actual" mode, `annualIncome` / `annualCategoryBudget` use the Plan+Actual rule (§4).
- Verdict: `surplus ≥ 0` → "Feasible — $X/yr surplus"; else "Short — $X/yr gap." **This is the annual plan**, not "can I afford this right now." Point users to Cash flow for timing.

## 6. Cash flow through the year

Running balance, month by month:

```
gh           = Σ monthly of FIXED goals (non-category, non-residual, non-archived)   // leaves spendable cash
balance[0]   = startCash
balance[m]   = balance[m-1] + (income[m] − expense[m] − gh − oneTime[m])   // income/expense per §4
lowest point = min over m   → the month spendable cash dips the most
```

Goal contributions **are** subtracted here: money moved into a savings goal stays in your net worth but is no longer *spendable cash*. For **completed** months this uses the **actual** goal movements from the ledger (`ag`) — so a skipped or doubled contribution shows up in real spendable cash — for the **current** month `max(actual, planned)`, and for **future** months the planned fixed goal hold. `oneTime[m]` carries a one-off outflow (e.g. a modeled purchase) in its month. `renderStress` (the live Cash-flow screen) calls this exact function rather than rebuilding its own arrays.

Verdict: if the lowest balance `< 0`, savings run dry that month (warn); if a **Nest Egg Floor** is set (§7a) and the low falls below it, warn with the shortfall. Otherwise "Covered." This model — not the annual plan — answers "will a big bill land before I've saved for it?"

The live cash-flow screen and the Decision Engine (§12) share the exact same function (`computeCashflow(plan, opts)`), so a scenario's numbers can never disagree with what the screen shows. **Presentation (this is projected cash, not the contingency, not necessarily cash-on-hand).** The running-total column is labelled **"Projected cash"** (not "Balance"), because it is `startCash + Σ net` — a projection, not a live account balance. When **Starting savings is $0** the screen shows a conspicuous note that the figures are a cash-flow *trend* from an artificial $0 baseline, not the money actually on hand (set Starting savings for a true projected balance). And because a positive projected-cash trend does **not** refill an overspent **contingency** (a separate pool — coverage never touches cash flow, §contingency), the verdict surfaces the contingency alongside: overspent → "projected cash is not your contingency … a positive trend doesn't refill it"; in the black → a neutral "separate from your contingency ($X available there)". Invariants: `verify-cashflow-presentation.js`.

**Surplus allocation — the inverse of expense coverage (`monthlyCashReconciliation`).** Expense coverage answers "where does an OVERAGE come from" (category → envelope → contingency → cash). The inverse question — when a month's ACTUAL income exceeds what the month required, where does the surplus go — now has an authoritative, **computed (non-writing)** helper. Hierarchy: **actual income → actual consumption → protected fixed/category-linked goal commitments → REPAIR the contingency deficit → residual (% of leftover) goals → unallocated reserves.** Exact reconciliation identity (proven in `verify-cash-allocation.js`): `income + reserveDraw === consumption + protectedGoals + contingencyRepair + residualFunding + unallocated`. Inputs are canonical: `income`/`consumption` from `monthActualTotals` (excludes goal moves, includes unbudgeted, refunds net consumption down), protected goals from actual fixed/linked contributions. **Critical no-double-count rule:** only the contingency deficit **entering** the month is repairable from that month's surplus — the current month's own buffer overspend is already inside `consumption`, so repairing the current end-of-month deficit would subtract it twice. The entering position is the pool balance at the end of the prior month, which is exactly `rawBuffer` (it excludes the in-progress current month); `_contingencyEntering(mo)` reads it from `getContingencyFacts().history` for any month. Like the coverage layer, this writes nothing — it says how a surplus *would* restore the pool. Policy (settled): contingency repair comes **before** residual goals (a residual goal is "% of what's left," and a negative shared cushion is part of what must be repaired before there is truly anything left), while fixed goals stay protected first. Wren consumes this for the "projected cash → where does it go?" conversation. Wren consumes this for the "projected cash → where does it go?" conversation, and the **Contingency card draws it**: a "where this month's cash is going" waterfall (deposits → spending → protected goals → surplus → contingency repaired → residual → remaining) plus a one-line interpretation, and a RAW / repaired / EFFECTIVE contingency distinction so a historical negative buffer doesn't read as "the surplus didn't matter". The card renders straight from `monthlyCashReconciliation()` — no math is duplicated in the view — and appears only once a deposit has landed this month (`cashReconCardHTML`). Invariants: `verify-cash-allocation.js`, `verify-wren-cash-alloc.js`, `verify-cash-recon-card.js`. **Optional contingency TARGET:** after repairing the deficit to $0, surplus builds a positive cushion toward a user-set `meta.contingencyTarget` before residual goals (hierarchy repair → build → residual → unallocated; identity extended with `contingencyBuild`; target 0 leaves everything unchanged). The Contingency card presents the state as **available-now (effective, floored at $0) / raw history / covered-by-this-month's-surplus / effective**, keeping raw history and planning-effective strictly separate (nothing is written). **All surfaces speak from one reconciled description** (`contingencyReconNote`/`contingencyReconSentence`): the Cash-Flow verdict, the Month buffer-over coverage line, Wren's deposit answer, and the Contingency card all say "raw contingency is −$X, but this month's surplus can repair $R of it (advisory — not moved automatically)" when a surplus exists, and only keep the "comes out of cash / rebuild via under-budget months" language when there genuinely is no surplus — so no surface contradicts the card. Invariants: `verify-cross-surface-recon.js`. Deferred: Decision-Engine surfacing, and floor-aware multi-month reconciliation.

## 7. Available / leftover cash

Two distinct values; never present the average as "available this month":

- **Average free cash** (`residualPool()`) = `max(0, annualIncome − annualCategoryBudget − annualFixedGoalSavings) ÷ 12`. A conceptual monthly *average*. Wren phrases affordability from this and explicitly calls it an average, not a guarantee.
- **Uncommitted this month** (Wren's "find money") = `incomeReceivedThisMonth − spentThisMonth − Σ max(0, mbud − mspent)` (income in, minus spent, minus what's still earmarked for the rest of this month's budgeted categories).

The "bump this goal" nudge is gated on `residualPool()` (annual average), so its copy says **"your annual budget has room,"** not "you have room this month."

## 8. Envelopes / banked (contingency)

For an envelope-rolling category:

```
banked = carryIn + Σ over COMPLETED months (mbud − actualSpend)
```

The **current month is deliberately excluded** — its unused budget isn't banked until the month closes (otherwise "savings" would balloon on the 1st before expenses are incurred). Label it **"banked through <last completed month>,"** never just "banked." Overspent envelopes may go **negative** (overspending genuinely consumes the cushion); only "progress toward an upcoming save-up" floors at 0.

**Rollover starts when the category is designated, not at budget adoption.** When a category is switched to envelope/buffer, `setCatRoll` stamps `cfg.rollStart` (YYYYMM). Rollover then counts only from the later of budget adoption and `rollStart` — both the current-year sum (`rollStartFloor`) and prior-year carry (`catCarryStartYM`, applied to `loadCarryIn`'s spend *and* budget sides). So flipping 2026 Dining to an envelope in January 2027 doesn't retroactively bank its unused 2026 budget. Categories with no `rollStart` recorded keep their prior behavior (roll from adoption). The catch-up "set aside $X/mo" for an upcoming annual bill uses **inclusive** periods (current month through due, `mu+1`), matching the goal model (§9).

**Consumption vs budget used.** Two distinct per-category numbers: `mspent` is **consumption** (real spending, goal moves excluded — the "how much did I spend" figure), and `mused` (`budUsed(c)`) is **budget used** = consumption **plus** any category-linked goal contribution, because that money consumed the category's allocation even though it isn't spending. Everything that answers "how much room is left" — the category card's "$X left", over-budget flags, S2S, and the envelope balance (`catBalance`) — uses `mused`; Insights and "how much did I spend" use `mspent`.

**As-of cutoff, everywhere.** One `_asOfOK(r)` (date ≤ today, matching the sheet's Spent formulas) gates *every* actuals path — `actualIE`, `monthActualTotals`, `buildIndexes`, `catSpend12`, `billSpentMonth`, `catMonthly`, `goalContribMonth`, and goal balances — so a future-dated entry can't count on one screen and not another. Fixed-goal saving in Plan+Actual uses **actual** contributions for completed months (`agf`), so a doubled or skipped one registers in annual feasibility, not just cash flow. `renderFeas` consumes the shared `computeAnnualPlan` rather than rebuilding its own blend.

**One pooled balance, one bill.** A "you've saved $X of $Y for <bill>" claim (`envUpcoming` / the contingency card) is only made when the itemized category holds **exactly one** annual/save-up bill (`sole`). When several annual bills share one envelope ($1,500 banked toward Auto $1,200 *and* Home $1,800), the balance can't be split per bill, so the card shows only the next due date. Never sum **envelope + buffer + linked-goal** balances into a single "banked" total unless each represents distinct underlying cash — envelope money is earmarked to a category, buffer is a shared cushion, and a goal held inside an asset account is already counted in that account.

**Sinking-fund transparency (v0.68.42) — DISPLAY ONLY, never a deduction.** In the reserve breakdown's "What's ahead" (§0c/§6), each **logged known bill** in an **envelope** category now shows how much of it is *already banked* in that envelope: *"$1,500 already banked in your Tuition envelope — already part of your available cash above; $4,500 of this bill isn't set aside yet."* Computed in `reserveBreakdown()` (actual-year only): for each envelope category, `catBalance(c)` (floored at 0) is attributed to that category's upcoming known-bill detail lines **earliest-month first** (larger bill first within a month), so **a banked dollar is credited to at most one bill and is never shown funding two obligations** (the same discipline as `sole`/`multi` above). **This changes the EXPLANATION only — it never touches `computeCashflow`, the reserve, or `goalSafeToMove`.** The reason is the §7b single-source identity: the banked balance is *unspent budget that never left the account*, so it is **already inside `available cash now`**, and the future bill draws it down through the projected path. Subtracting `bill − banked` from the reserve as well would count the banked dollars twice (the exact double-count this feature was asked to avoid). The invariant is enforced empirically: `verify-sinking-transparency.js` asserts `safeToGoal` is **byte-identical envelope-vs-plain** while the note renders correctly, and two mutations (over-attribute; reuse-a-dollar) are caught in `verify-mutation.js`.

**Genuine remaining limitation (documented, not yet fixed).** The transparency layer credits *already-banked* dollars correctly, but a **future** monthly sinking-fund contribution is still treated as ordinary recurring baseline by the hybrid forecast (§6, `_cfFutureOut`). If you budget $500/mo in Sep–Nov *as savings toward* a $6,000 Dec bill, those future $500 baselines are subtracted as recurring spend **and** the $6,000 bill lands in December — a mild over-reservation for the pre-bill months (it errs toward reserving *more*, the safe direction). NestWorth cannot infer that a particular future $500 budget exists *for* that specific December obligation; the recurring baseline is deliberately purpose-blind. **Closing this is a separate semantic enhancement, not a correction to the already-banked cash math.**

**Sinking-fund DATA MODEL (v0.68.43, foundation — built, inert).** The first slice of the link work is the schema, on `state.meta.cats[k]` (all optional; absent = legacy = "ongoing"): `purpose` ("ongoing"/"sinking"/"split"); `contribution` (monthly $ **assigned** toward a linked obligation — a reservation of existing cash, not extra spend; for "split", consumption = budget − contribution); and `sinking` = `{id, obligation:{id,name,amount,dueYear,dueMonth,source}, openingFunded, openingISO}` — a link to a specific obligation by **stable id** (never name-matched, so a rename/amount/date edit doesn't break it), capturing the banked balance **at link time** as an opening figure (never fabricating past monthly contributions). Read helpers (`catPurpose`, `catContributionMonthly`, `catConsumptionMonthly`, `catSinking`, `newSinkId`, `captureOpeningFunded`) clamp defensively — `contribution` is forced into `[0, this month's budget]`, `purpose` to the enum — so a bad stored value can never reach an engine. `sinkingSummary(name)` is the review read-model: `alreadyBanked` (the LIVE envelope balance, already inside available cash), `plannedContributionsBeforeDueDate` (contribution × months from now up to but not including the due month), `expectedFundedAtDueDate` (capped at the obligation), `remainingUnfundedAtDueDate`. **Crucially this layer is PROSPECTIVE and INERT: no engine reads any of it yet, so its presence is byte-for-byte neutral** across safe-to-move, cash flow, net worth, envelope balances and the reserve — proven in `verify-sinking-foundation.js`, and the fields survive the meta save/merge/load round-trip untouched (`normMeta` keeps `cats` wholesale; `_mergeMap` merges whole cat objects). Note `catCfg` returns the *meta config* (roll/purpose/contribution) while `bud12` lives on the *category object* in `state.cats` — the helpers read each from its correct home (`_catObj` for budget). Budget in the plan year is still the full `bud12`; the consumption/contribution split does not yet reach `_cfFutureOut` — that is the engine increment.

**Sinking-fund FORECAST ENGINE (v0.68.44, increment 2 — built).** The forward projection now *acts* on a linked sinking fund. `_cfSinkFree(plan,m)` returns the total monthly `contribution` to treat as a **reservation of existing cash** (not recurring spend) for month `m` — but *only* for a category that is explicitly linked (`catSinking`) **and** classified "sinking"/"split" (never inferred from a name or a bare contribution value), only within this plan year, and only in the months from now up to but not including the obligation's due month (the due month is when the bill itself is the outflow). `computeCashflow` subtracts it from the current month's planned outflow (through `max(actualOut, adjustedPlan)`, so genuine actual spending still wins) and from each future month's baseline in `_cfFutureOut`. Because the reservation is simply *not subtracted as spend*, that cash stays in the projected path and the bill draws it down there — the same mechanism by which an already-banked balance funds a bill (§ transparency), now extended to the *future* contributions that were the real double-count. Consequences, all proven in `verify-sinking-engine.js`: safe-to-move rises by **exactly** the newly-recognized funded portion (`= plannedContributionsBeforeDueDate`) when the linked bill is the binding forward low; the §7b operational boundary still holds on the higher figure (move exactly safe → forward low on the floor; +$1 breaches); a bill small enough to be absorbed creates no new low (ENV-FC-002); removing the link reverts to ordinary treatment (ENV-FC-006); a later due date frees more months (monotonic). **Gating = zero regression:** `_cfSinkFree` returns 0 for every legacy/unlinked category, so the forecast is byte-identical without a real link. It is written with `typeof` guards so `computeCashflow`/`_cfFutureOut` extracted in isolation (golden-suite / golden-invariants) safely see 0. Two nuances worth stating: the reinterpretation runs in **actual mode only** (the current plan year, where safe-to-move lives) — a future-year plan keeps ordinary treatment for now; and the current month is reinterpreted through the plan side of `max()`, so if the household has *actually spent* the money this month, that real spend still counts (a reservation only frees budget that hasn't been consumed).

**Forecast Checkup (v0.68.45, increment 3 — built): review-and-confirm migration for existing users.** Nothing is auto-converted; every existing envelope/budget stays valid under today's semantics until the user classifies it. `forecastCheckup()` is a READ-ONLY scan surfacing three buckets: envelopes that may be saving for a specific bill (`checkupBillCandidates()`), budgets that look **lumpy** (`lumpyCategories()` — a single logged month ≥ 2× the median of the others and materially larger, the one-time spike that distorts an average baseline; the −$36K guard), and ongoing spending envelopes. A **candidate bill is a save-ahead lump, not routine spend** (v0.68.48): it must be due in a *future* month (`mo > cm` — the current month's bills are already covered by the current budget) **and** exceed that month's category budget (a bill within budget is ordinary spend); expense rows only, so a goal move or deposit is never an obligation (ENV-FC-007); earliest-due first. Each candidate carries its identifying **metadata** (due date, payee, amount) so the card shows a "The bill" table rather than a bare figure. Matches are *suggested*, never auto-applied, and each prompt (match or lumpy) the user answers is recorded in **`meta.checkupDone`** (a new top-level meta field, threaded through `normMeta`/`_metaBase`/`writeMeta`) so the scan drops it and it never nags again. `checkupLinkDiff()` shows the before/after safe-to-move a link would produce through a **dry run** (`_checkupSafeWith` patches the cfg, reads `goalSafeToMove`, then restores the exact prior cfg — nothing persists). Committing is `checkupSetEnvelope()` (sets purpose/contribution/link, defaults a pure sinking link's contribution to the whole envelope budget so the committed safe-to-move **equals the diff the user was shown**, and captures the opening-funded balance at link time) / `checkupUnlink()` (removes the link, keeps the envelope and its balance — never redirects money, ENV-FC-006). **The governing invariant, proven in `verify-forecast-checkup.js`: the scan changes nothing, and a classification moves no cash — net worth, envelope balances, the ledger and history are untouched; only the forecast (and thus safe-to-move) moves, by exactly the amount the diff promised.** `forecastCheckupHTML()` renders the review card; `renderCheckup()` mounts it into `#checkupSlot` on the Budget tab. **Every button carries no inline data — actions are stashed by integer index and fired via `ckLink`/`ckDismiss`/`ckAdjust`** (the earlier version inlined `JSON.stringify(...)` inside a double-quoted `onclick`, which was malformed HTML and silently dead on device). The handlers persist through the `writeMeta` contract and re-render: `doCheckupLink` links and marks the match done; `doCheckupDismiss(kind,cat)` records a "Not now"/"That's normal" answer; `doCheckupAdjust(cat)` opens the category editor (`openCatEdit`) to fix a lumpy budget and marks it answered.

**Envelope coverage view (v0.68.50).** "View envelopes" (inside the Month money card) shows, under each envelope, the **upcoming bills it is covering** with a per-bill covered/short mark. `envelopeUpcoming(name)` is a READ-ONLY display helper: bills come from the envelope's linked obligations (`catObligations`) if any, else the upcoming logged expense rows in that category (this month forward, expenses only); it then walks them earliest-due-first, funding each from a running pool seeded at `max(0, catBalance)` — so a banked dollar covers the *nearest* bill first and never funds two — and marks each **covered** (funded in full) or **short** (with the remaining "$X to go"), plus a roll-up ("$X still needed across N upcoming bills" / "all N covered"). **Multi-obligation sinking funds (v0.68.47 — built; the sinking-fund feature set is now complete).** One envelope can fund several future bills. The link stores `obligations[]`; `catObligations(name)` normalizes the single-or-array shape and sorts **earliest-due-first** (the deterministic allocation order), and `catSinking` accepts either shape (single links from v0.68.44 keep working). The engine `_cfSinkFree` generalizes cleanly: for each linked sinking/split category, a month `m` (from now forward) frees its contribution **iff** some linked obligation is due *after* `m` (the fund is still saving toward a later bill) **and** no obligation is due *in* `m` (there the bill itself is the outflow, and the hybrid `max(bud, bill)` already subsumes the contribution — freeing it too would understate the bill). The whole sinking-fund arc — data model, forecast engine, review UI, and multi-obligation — is complete. Design captured for the next phase: the link points to a stable obligation id (`envelope.linkedObligationId` / a `fundingLinks[]` table for one-envelope→many-bills), created only on explicit user confirmation (event-driven suggestion when a future bill is logged or an envelope is created — never auto-linked, never silently redirected if the bill is deleted). Once linked, the forecast must reinterpret the *path* (future contributions become reservations of existing cash, not additional outflows — so a banked/contributed dollar and the bill are never both counted) rather than adding another subtraction, exposing `alreadyBanked / plannedContributionsBeforeDueDate / expectedFundedAtDueDate / remainingUnfundedAtDueDate`, with deterministic earliest-due-first allocation when one envelope funds several bills. The governing invariant for that phase: **linking must change the *allocation/explanation* of available cash, never manufacture or destroy household cash** — `safeToGoal` may move only by the genuinely-newly-recognized funded portion when that obligation is the binding forward low.

**Contingency is a shared SECONDARY cushion, applied as a computed coverage layer (`computeRollover`).** The financial hierarchy is **category budget → accumulated envelope → shared contingency → uncovered deficit**. Raw envelope balances (`catBalance`, incl. negatives) are **preserved for accountability** — a red envelope stays red, its history is never erased or netted to zero. On top of the raw balances, `computeRollover` computes how much of the overspend the contingency actually covers, so *availability* and *history* are two separate numbers (the same split as consumption vs budget-used). This is a **computed allocation of existing money — nothing is written to the ledger, no transaction, no transfer** (a manual transfer would fabricate a movement and reintroduce the classification headaches we removed). **Coverage capacity and total uncovered shortfall are two different things.** Buffer categories are **pooled first** (a +$2,000 and a −$500 buffer category net to a +$1,500 `rawBuffer`, not "$2,000 available plus a $500 deficit"). Then: `rawBuffer ≥ 0 → availableBuffer = rawBuffer, bufferDeficit = 0`; `rawBuffer < 0 → availableBuffer = 0` (**never negative coverage — a negative buffer must never shrink another envelope's coverage**) **and** `bufferDeficit = |rawBuffer|` — the overspent pool is a shortfall of its own. A negative buffer category feeds `rawBuffer`/`bufferDeficit` **only, never `envelopeDeficit`**, so nothing is double-counted. Fields: `rawBuffer` (pooled, may be negative, preserved for the UI); `availableBuffer = max(0, rawBuffer)`; `bufferDeficit = max(0, −rawBuffer)`; `totalEnvelopeDeficit = Σ |min(0, rawBalance)|` over **envelopes only**; `contingencyUsed = min(availableBuffer, totalEnvelopeDeficit)`; `uncoveredDeficit` (= `uncoveredEnvelopeDeficit`) `= totalEnvelopeDeficit − contingencyUsed`; `remainingContingency = max(0, availableBuffer − contingencyUsed)`; **`totalUncoveredDeficit = uncoveredDeficit + bufferDeficit`** — the household's whole rollover shortfall; and per envelope `rawBalance`, `coveredByContingency`, `uncovered`. Canonical case: raw buffer −$1,000 + Children −$2,000 → $0 coverage, $2,000 uncovered envelope, $1,000 buffer deficit, **$3,000 total** (not $2,000). Wren distinguishes the four: "how much contingency do I have?" → `remainingContingency`; "is my contingency overspent?" → `rawBuffer`/`bufferDeficit`; "how much of my envelopes isn't covered?" → `uncoveredDeficit`; "how much rollover shortfall altogether?" → `totalUncoveredDeficit`. **Allocation across multiple negatives is deterministic**: proportional to each deficit, sorted largest-first with a name tiebreak, and the last envelope absorbs the rounding remainder so per-envelope coverage **reconciles to `contingencyUsed` exactly, to the cent**. Coverage **never** alters consumption, budget-used, goal funding, cash flow, or net worth — it is reallocation of an existing cushion, not a flow. Every surface that speaks to availability (Contingency card, the "Can you cover it?" so-what, Wren) consumes these facts; the this-month fixed overage is measured against `remainingContingency` (what's left after covering cumulative envelope deficits), not the raw buffer. Invariants: `verify-contingency-coverage.js`. Wren narrates these facts for "how much contingency is available?", "how much is uncovered?", "which envelopes are using contingency?", and "why is X still negative if contingency covers it?". **Temporal questions are a separate contract.** `computeRollover` is a snapshot; a *time* question ("when did my contingency become overspent?", "when was it last positive?", "how has it changed since January?") is answered from **`getContingencyFacts()`**, which returns the current state PLUS a month-by-month pool history — carry-in baseline, each completed month's balance, `firstNegativeMonth` (the month the pooled buffer first crossed from ≥0 to <0), `lastPositiveMonth`+balance, and the biggest single-month drop. History accumulates over **completed months only**, the same horizon `catBalance` uses, so `history[last].bal === rawBuffer` by construction. Wren **narrates** this (never re-derives the crossing herself); the router checks temporal language (when / what month / first / last / became / went / since / before it) **before** current-state contingency intent so "WHEN did it become overspent" is not answered as "IS it overspent". The plain state question ("how much is in my contingency after my bills?") is answered cleanly (balance + available + why) and deliberately does **not** fold in uncovered-envelope totals or year-end envelope risk — those are their own questions. Invariants: `verify-contingency-history.js`. **The same history is also drawn on the Contingency card** (`contingencyTrendHTML`) — a compact bar sparkline of the month-end pooled balance across completed months, green above the zero line and red below, with the first-negative month highlighted and a plain caption ("Went negative in July …" / "In the black every month"). It reads the identical `getContingencyFacts()` history Wren narrates, so the drawn trend and Wren's spoken answer cannot disagree. Invariants: `verify-contingency-trend.js`. **Wren also corrects the causal premise "despite my deposit, why no contingency?"** — a deposit does NOT build the contingency pool; only unspent buffer-category budget banks there, while income/deposits go to cash and net worth (a separate place). So a large deposit can't create or refill contingency; rebuilding it needs buffer categories to come in under budget. (Guarded to require a deposit/income term + a contingency term + a why/despite/negation framing, so a plain "will my paycheck cover the overage?" still routes to the cash/coverage answer.)

**"Can you cover it?" — the over-budget so-what (`coverageReport` / `coverageHtml`, mirrored by Wren).** An over-budget flag without a verdict is only half an answer. `coverageReport()` reconciles the overspend against the banked cushions, in three parts: (1) **This month, non-envelope overspend** (`fixedTot` = Σ `budUsed − mbud` over reset-monthly categories that are over — money with no buffer behind it) vs the **contingency buffer** and, secondarily, **genuine under-budget slack in OTHER plain categories** (`underRoom` = Σ `mbud − budUsed` over reset-monthly categories that are under): covered outright / covered with the buffer + slack / short by the remainder (comes from cash). `underRoom` counts **plain categories only** — an envelope's or buffer's underspend banks into its own cushion (envelope balance / the shared-contingency pool) and is credited there, so counting it here too would double-cover the same dollar. This offset is why an exactly-offsetting month (Living +$100, Dining −$100) reads as *covered*, not "$100 from cash". **Supportable-goal funding also charges UNBUDGETED consumption.** `goalFundingStatus()` decides how much of an over-budget month erodes residual "% of leftover" capacity, and its cushioned inputs (`fixedTot`/`underRoom`, envelope self-coverage, contingency) are all budget-line based — so spending with *no budget line at all* (a category NestWorth isn't budgeting, or an uncategorized expense) would be invisible to it even though canonical `monthActualTotals` counts it as real consumption. `unbudgetedConsumption()` sums this month's expense rows that aren't in any budgeted category (uncategorized included; credits/refunds net down; goal movements excluded via `isExpenseRow`), and it's folded into the no-cushion cash pressure alongside the plain overage, net of genuine plain under-budget room. Budgeted categories are skipped (their spend is already in `mspent`/`budUsed`), so nothing is double-charged, and the household cash identity still reconciles with **C = canonical actual consumption (including unbudgeted)**. Invariants: `verify-goal-funding.js` (GF-013…GF-018), `verify-goal-funding-reconcile.js`. (2) **Envelopes over THIS month** (`spent[cm] > bud12[cm]`) are surfaced *because* the plain over-budget flag skips them (§preamble) — each shown as drawing its banked balance down (still fine while `catBalance ≥ 0`, "in the red" once negative). (3) **Year-end solvency**: each envelope projected to Dec at its **year-to-date pace** (`catBalance + Σ remaining bud12 − avg-monthly-spend × months-left`); any that ends negative is flagged as running short before year-end (`allSolvent` = none do). Everything past the current month is labelled "at your current pace" — a projection, never a promise.

## 9. Goals

- **Balance** = `startBal + Σ` goal-movement amounts for the goal.
- **Funding months** `goalFundMonths(targetDate)` = `monthsUntil(targetDate) + 1` = the current month **through** the target month, inclusive ("due by end of the target month"). A goal set up this month counts this month.
- **Auto monthly** (when the user leaves it blank) = `target ÷ goalFundMonths`.
- **Pace** = `need ÷ goalFundMonths`, where `need = target − balance`. "Behind" if the planned monthly `<` pace.
- **Goal ETA** (Wren) = `ceil(need ÷ monthly)` months from now → projected month.
- **Anti-double-counting:** a goal whose money physically sits inside an asset account is *earmarked within that account*, never added on top. `AccountBalance = Available + Σ EarmarkedGoals`. Net worth uses the account balance once.
- **Residual (leftover-funded) goals** (`computeResidual`) draw from the average free-cash pool (`residualPool()`, §7). A goal with an explicit `residualPct` takes that share of the pool; goals left blank split whatever the percentages don't claim ("rest"); with no percentages set at all, the pool splits evenly. A residual goal stops drawing once it reaches its target (so it can't keep starving the others). This is uncapped by default — an 80% goal draws 80% of the pool however large that is.
  - **Cent reconciliation (nonnegative).** When percentages fill the pool, the rounding remainder is distributed a cent at a time across goals with a **positive** allocation (largest first), **never taking any goal below $0** — so a 0%-share (or tiny) residual goal can never receive a negative contribution from a rounding remainder. (Property audit fix: the old "remainder → last array element" rule produced −$0.01 when the last goal had a 0% share.) Invariant: `verify-property.js` throws 4,000 randomized households at the real `computeResidual`, asserting allocations are never negative, never overdraw the pool, and reconcile to exactly the pool when fully allocated.
  - **Optional monthly cap** (`residualCap`, dollars/month): the goal takes its percentage of the pool but **never more than the cap** (`_resMo = min(pool × pct, cap)`). The amount above the cap is **not** redistributed to other residual goals — it stays spendable (a cap means "don't put more than this in," not "move it elsewhere"). When any goal is capped the pool is deliberately under-allocated, so the "hand the rounding remainder to the last goal" step is skipped (it would silently exceed a cap or over-draw the pool). `_resCapped`/`_resUncapped` are exposed for the UI; `_resMode` gains a ", capped" suffix in a month where the cap actually bites. **Invariants:** `verify-goal-cap.js` — 80% of a $2,000 pool caps to $800; a lean $500 pool draws $400 uncapped; a co-goal keeps its own 20% and does not absorb the capped goal's excess; the excess stays spendable; save→load round-trips `residualCap`.

## 9b. Goal facts for Wren (`getGoalFacts`)

Wren is a **narrator, not a calculator**. For any goal question she reads one authoritative object — `getGoalFacts(query)` — and never re-derives a goal number by hand. Every field comes from an engine helper: `balance`/`mtd` from `loadGoals`, `ytd` from `goalContribMonth` (per month, as-of-respecting), `plannedMonthly` = `g._resMo` for a residual goal (via `computeResidual`) else `g.monthly`, `monthsToTarget` from `goalFundMonths`, `requiredPace` = `remaining ÷ monthsToTarget`, `behind` when required pace > planned, `eta` projected from planned monthly, `status` ∈ reached/behind/overdue/on_pace/unfunded/archived. **Retrieval is explicit**: no match → `{found:false}`; two equally-good matches → `{found:false, ambiguous:true, candidates:[…]}` so Wren *asks* instead of guessing; the router pulls the literal "my <X> goal" phrase so "car" (Car Fund vs Car Loan) is caught as ambiguous and "my Yacht goal" as not-found. `wrenGoalNarrate(facts, question)` is pure formatting — it picks the sub-answer (balance / remaining / pace+ETA / this-month funding / YTD / why-behind) with no arithmetic.

**The Wren golden suite** (`verify-wren-golden.js`) is separate from the financial golden suite and answers a different question: not "can NestWorth calculate?" but "can Wren understand what it calculated and explain it without saying something financially nonsensical?" One fixed synthetic household; questions across goals / income / spending / cash flow / net worth / comparisons / adversarial; each answer checked on five dimensions — **Retrieval** (right entity), **Classification** (right concept: spend vs goal-funding vs cash flow vs net worth), **Computation** (number came from an engine — enforced by spying that goal answers call `getGoalFacts`), **Interpretation** (explanation follows from the numbers), **Boundaries** (declines/asks when the data isn't there). Golden Test #1 is the exact residual-cap car-goal exchange that failed in production. Extend this suite before trusting any new Wren answer path.

**Wren capabilities (v0.66) — the semantic-regression surface `verify-wren-golden.js` guards (66 assertions, 14 domains × 5 dimensions, WREN-NNN ids, spy-verified computation, cross-surface fact invariants):** entity resolution (`getGoalFacts` — exact name wins, else longest full-name substring, else shared-word → ambiguous ask; the router matches full names before shared words so "Car Fund" never resolves to "Emergency Fund" via the word "fund"); **false-premise correction** (a question asserting something untrue — "why did I spend $700 on Travel" when consumption was $300, "why did net worth fall" when it rose, "why did I miss my Vacation contribution" when it was made, "contingency erased my overspending" — is corrected from authoritative facts, never explained as if true); **epistemic boundaries** (markets, future asset values, un-modeled future bills, another person's purchase intent, "was it necessary", "will I definitely…" are declined or hedged, never turned into false certainty); **product-behaviour boundary** (Wren is the guide, not the developer — never "I can add a button / I'll implement"); **income cadence** (checks per month, budgetable-per-check, set-aside per-check vs per-month, why a 3-paycheck month is higher — from the paycheck config and real-payday calendar); **budget-used vs consumption** and **refund-is-not-income** classification; **linked-goal net-worth** earmarking; **income-drop what-if** routed through the Decision Engine (a $X/mo income drop == a $X/mo recurring expense for surplus/cash/floor); and **deep conversational memory** (`wrenCtx = {goal, cat, envelope, why, scenario}`, `wrenFollowup`): a bare **"why?"** substantiates the last CLAIM from authoritative facts (`why` descriptor: goal-behind / over-budget / net-worth-change / low-cash); a **carried hypothetical** ("increase Car Fund to $1,000" then "would that put us below our floor?" / "and our cash?" / "what about the surplus?") re-runs the stored `scenario` through the Decision Engine and answers the specific sub-question; a **category pronoun** follow-up ("how much is left there?" after a category answer) resolves `wrenCtx.cat`; **pace follow-ups** ("am I on track?") and pronoun goal edits ("increase it to $1,000") resolve `wrenCtx.goal`. Every follow-up is gated hard — it never fires when the question names another entity or uses spend/decision words. The suite (`verify-wren-golden.js`) now spans **127 assertions across 17 domains × 5 dimensions** (including a Chain domain that spy-verifies the carried hypothetical re-ran the engine), plus new answers for debt level/paydown, internal-transfer net-worth, month-over-month comparison, year-end projection, new-recurring-cost what-ifs, and goal withdrawals.

**Conversational memory is single-slot per entity type by deliberate design (not a referent stack), and the suite documents its exact contract (`Memory` domain, WREN-330–337).** SUPPORTED: a direct follow-up to the last goal or the last category resolves from `wrenCtx`; switching goal→category or category→goal is driven by the *fresh* explicit entity, which always overrides remembered context; "increase it to $X" one turn later resolves the last goal; the latest CLAIM overwrites `wrenCtx.why`, so a bare "why?" always substantiates the most recent statement, never a stale one. BOUNDARY (correct financial-safety behavior): a NEW ambiguous reference ("car goal" with three car goals) **asks which** rather than silently reusing the remembered goal — a finance app must never attach a pronoun to the wrong financial object and answer confidently about the wrong thing. There is no time-based expiration; context is overwritten by the next resolved entity/claim. A full ordered referent stack (for "compare Vacation and Car Fund", "the first one", "go back to Vacation") is intentionally deferred — it would be built as explicit conversational state (type, canonical name, turn number, role), not more regex, and only when real usage produces repeated multi-entity failures. **Release reporting** is coverage-by-capability, not raw count, on two axes that answer different questions and are never recombined into one gate. **By capability**, each reported separately so a regression instantly reveals whether it's a harmless conversational limit or a financial-trust failure: Goals · Spending · Income · Cash Flow/Decisions · Worth/Debt · Contingency · **Entity Resolution · Follow-up Resolution · Conversation Chains · Memory/Referent State · False-Premise Correction · Epistemic Boundaries · Product Behavior** (the last seven deliberately split apart — False-Premise and Epistemic are Wren's core trust metrics and must be visible on their own). **By quality dimension**: Retrieval · Classification · Computation · Interpretation · Boundaries. **Overall** reports both cases (a WREN-NNN passes only if all its assertions pass) and assertions. A **release-bar breach** is flagged per-item, never averaged: the trust capabilities (Entity Resolution, False-Premise, Epistemic, Product, **As-of/Future-Date, Provenance**) and the critical dimensions (Retrieval, Computation, Classification, Boundaries) must each be 100%.

**The suite is a release-grade oracle, hardened against false-green (three harness rules).** (1) **No false-green** — if an assertion's description names an expected amount/date/entity/status/engine result, it checks that EXACT authoritative fact (`has(answer, money(F.value))`), computed by the real engine; semantic-regex is used only where there is no number to check (e.g. "declines to predict the market"). Financial Computation tests never accept "|| any dollar amount". (2) **Fixture isolation** — fixture factories (`makeBaseHousehold`, `makeContingencyHousehold`) rebuild the household and RESTORE every monkey-patched core function (`catSpend12`, `catLinkedGoal12`, `adoptionFloor`, `rollStartFloor`, `carryInFor`) captured pristine at start, so no domain leaks into another; standalone `run()` resets the full Wren context and spies before each question (paraphrase clusters are therefore independent), and only Chain/Follow-up/Memory turns deliberately carry state via `runK()`. (3) **Provenance, not just invocation** — the call-count spies prove Wren CALLED an engine; separate **sentinel** tests (WREN-400–404) prove she USED its returned value by stubbing `getGoalFacts`/`netWorthNow`/`planMetrics`/`computeRollover`/`evaluateDecision` with unmistakable numbers ($4,321.09, $42,424.24, −$55,555.55, $4,455.55, $44,444.44), asserting Wren echoes them, and restoring immediately. Failures print `{expected, actual, spy}`. Hardening the assertions this way surfaced four real Wren bugs (income-answer hijacking a spending comparison; "rollover shortfall altogether" misrouting to cash flow; "lowest cash month" not reporting a positive low; missing "what to save to finish by <month>") — fixed in v0.68.1.

**Resolve entities before intent.** When a message names a NestWorth entity that resolves uniquely (a goal like "Car Fund"), goal-domain intent outranks generic keywords — "leftover", "saved", "monthly", "money" must not pull a goal question into monthly-spending analytics. The router matches the goal first, then interprets the rest within the goal domain (80% → residual percentage, "leftover" → residual pool, $800 → configured monthly, "up to or past" → cap interaction). One word ("leftover") cannot decide the route: "How much did we have leftover in August?" → monthly finances; "Put 80% of leftover into Car Fund" → residual-goal behavior. **Residual-goal mechanics questions** ("is the $800 a cap?", "which controls, the $800 or the 80%?", "how much would Car Fund get?") are answered by **interrogating `computeResidual()`'s actual `_resMo` and the current `residualPool()`** — never by reading the goal's `monthly` setting and presenting it as the operative contribution. If `residualCap` is set, Wren says it's capped at that ceiling and gives the current draw vs the cap; if not, she says it is not capped, gives the actual current draw, and states the dollar figure isn't the operative rule under leftover funding.

## 10. Net worth

```
netWorth = totalAssets − totalDebts
```

Snapshots (per month) store **net, total assets, total debts, as-of date, and per-account composition**. A snapshot is taken when *composition* changes even if net is flat (e.g., $500 cash → $500 debt payoff leaves net unchanged but the position changed).

- **Change attribution** = `ΔNet = ΔAssets − ΔDebts`, where debt *reduction* is a positive contributor. Report the split: "Net worth grew $1,840 — +$800 assets, +$640 from paying down debt, +$400 other assets."
- **Freshness:** the figure is only as current as the last balance update. Show "updated N days ago" in the headline; flag once it's been > 45 days. Precision like `$184,220.37` must not imply live accuracy the data doesn't have.

## 7a. Nest Egg Floor

An optional per-budget number (`meta.floor`): the lowest the user wants their available cash to fall. It has no effect on any calculation — it's a **judgment line**. The cash-flow verdict and every Decision (§12) compare the lowest projected cash against it and report the margin ("$2,300 above your floor") or the breach ("$386 below your floor"). Blank/0 = not set = skipped.

## 7b. Safe to move to a goal (the answer panel — `goalSafeToMove`)

The join that makes NestWorth answer the user's actual question — *"how much can I move to my goal account today without any future month's projected cash falling below my Nest Egg Floor?"* — as **one number**, instead of six individually-correct numbers the user has to combine by hand.

**Where it appears (v0.68.49).** The figure leads BOTH surfaces where it's needed: the Month card ("Your money right now") and — because that is where the user actually allocates — the **Goals page** (`renderGoals` leads with a "Safe to move to your goals now" banner carrying the identical `goalSafeToMove().safeToGoal`, or $0 with the breach reason). Both read the one engine; neither recomputes, so they cannot disagree. Invariant: `verify-goals-safe-banner.js`.

**Single source of truth, no parallel math.** `goalSafeToMove()` does **not** re-derive cash. It runs the existing forward engine — `computeCashflow(currentPlan(), …)` (§6) — and reads its month-by-month `bal` path. That path already contains everything: future bills on their real dates, envelope/banked set-asides, the starting balance rolled forward, contingency effects, protected/linked/residual goal holds, and future income on real timing. Nothing about the projection is recomputed here and nothing is subtracted a second time.

**The definition is one line, and it's operational.** Let `forwardLow` = the lowest projected `bal` from the current month forward (off-year/plan mode looks across all 12 months; in the live year it scans from the current month to December). Let `floor` = the configured Nest Egg Floor (§7a), **not $0** unless the floor really is $0. Then

> `safeToGoal = max(0, forwardLow − floor)`

Why this is exactly right and not an approximation: moving `$X` out of household cash *today* is a one-time outflow in the current month, which shifts the **entire** forward balance curve down by `$X` uniformly. So the tightest future point — `forwardLow` — drops to `forwardLow − X`, and the largest `X` that keeps every future month `≥ floor` is precisely `forwardLow − floor`. This is **proven by simulation** in `verify-goal-safe.js`: clone the plan, set `oneTime[currentMonth] = safeToGoal`, re-run the full `computeCashflow`, and the new forward low lands **exactly on the floor**; `safeToGoal + $1` drives it one dollar below. No double-count is possible because the capacity is read off the same projection that already reflects every commitment — there is no separate "subtract the bills, subtract the envelopes, subtract the contingency" step to get wrong.

**Fields returned** (consumed identically by the Cash Flow panel `goalSafeHTML()` and by Wren): `safeToGoal`, `forwardLow` + `forwardLowMonth` (the tightest month, named), `floor`, `currentProjected`, `mustStay` (`currentProjected − safeToGoal`), `billsCovered` / `breach` / `breachBy` (is the plan already under the floor before any move?), and `startCashSet` / `startCash`. When Starting savings is $0 the panel and Wren both flag that the number is measured from an artificial $0 baseline (same caveat as §6). If the plan is already under the floor, `safeToGoal` is $0 and the panel warns rather than inviting a move. Invariants: `verify-goal-safe.js` (binding-boundary simulation, floor-awareness, single-source/no-double-count, breach, extra-income month, sensitivity, Wren-same-helper), plus a break-audited mutation (safe ignores the floor → uses $0).

## 12. The Decision Engine (scenarios)

The point of the whole architecture: run a hypothetical against the *entire* model deterministically, so Wren can narrate consequences instead of guessing them. All of it is pure (state-in / numbers-out); nothing touches the DOM or live data.

**Plan object** — a snapshot the models read: `{cons, cats, rows, goals, startCash, goalMonthlyFixed, oneTime[12]}`. `currentPlan()` builds it from live state; `clonePlan()` forks it.

**Pure models** (the same ones the live screens call):
- `computeAnnualPlan(plan)` → `{income, budget, goalSave, surplus}` (§5).
- `computeCashflow(plan, opts)` → month-by-month balances, `low`, `lowMonth`, `end` (§6).
- `goalCompletion(goal)` → projected finish month from `ceil((target−balance)/monthly)`.
- `netWorthNow()` → current position (§10) read from live accounts: `Σ assets + Σ stand-alone goal balances − Σ debts`.
- `computeNetWorthTrajectory(plan)` → `{now, end}` — projects net worth to year-end. It grows each remaining month by money **kept** = `income − expense − oneTime`. Goal contributions are **not** subtracted here (unlike cash flow §6): money moved into a goal *stays in net worth* — it's still yours, just no longer spendable. So accelerating a goal leaves this figure unchanged while it tightens cash; a purchase or a new recurring cost lowers it. Same current-month blend as §4.
- `planMetrics(plan)` → `{surplus, low, lowMonth, end, netEnd, netNow}` — the comparable summary (adds projected year-end net worth).

**`planMetrics`** — the Decision Engine's before/after summary — returns **two** annual-surplus figures: `surplus` is the **steady-state, ongoing** annual plan (plan mode), so a recurring what-if reads as its full ×12 run-rate (a $700/mo cost = −$8,400/yr); `surplusYr` is the **impact on this year** (Plan+Actual), where a change started mid-year only touches the remaining months (−$3,500 in August). The Decision table shows both, clearly labelled, rather than conflating them. A `goalMonthly` change only feeds the surplus/cash line for a **fixed** stand-alone goal; a **category-linked** goal (money already in its category budget) or a **residual** goal shows its finish-date shift but does not double-count as an extra commitment.

**Cash-flow outflow is one quantity.** `computeCashflow` compares **total** actual outflow (`ordinary expense + goal moves`) against **total** planned outflow (`category plan + fixed-goal hold`) with a single `max` in the current month — never maxing expense and goal separately, which would double-count a category-funded goal contribution (the $500 category plan and the $500 goal move are the same $500). `computeAnnualPlan` in actual mode adds each completed month's **category-linked** goal allocation (`agc`) back into budget, because that money consumed the category's plan.

**`evaluateDecision(change)`** clones the current plan, applies the change(s), reruns the models, and returns a structured before/after: `{type, batch, changes, feasible, before, after, goal?, floor, passesFloor}`. `feasible` = annual surplus ≥ 0 **and** the Nest Egg Floor holds. `change` may be a **single change object OR an array of changes** — an array is a *batch*: every change applies to one cloned plan and the whole model reruns once, so the before/after reflects all of them at once. `_applyChange(base, hypo, change, ctx)` applies one change to the hypo plan; expense/income concat onto `hypo` (not `base`) and one-time outflows *add* into their month, so batches compose correctly.

**Change types (one engine, many entrances):**
- `{type:'purchase', amount, month, from?}` — a one-time outflow. Reduces cash-flow low, year-end cash, and year-end net worth by the amount; annual surplus unchanged. `from:'goal:Name'` funds it from a savings goal: the portion the goal actually holds is spent from earmarked savings (`plan.goalSpend`, a **net-worth** reduction that leaves spendable cash untouched), and any **excess above the goal balance splits onto cash** (a one-time outflow in its month) rather than vanishing — so a $6,000 trip funded from a $3,000 goal drops net worth by the full $6,000 but cash by only $3,000, and `goal.fromGoal`/`goal.shortfall` report the split.
- `{type:'expenseMonthly', amount, months?}` — a recurring cost (e.g. a car payment). `months` (1-based) scopes it to chosen months; **absent = all year**, so the default annual-surplus impact is the steady-state `amount×12` (the structural recurring plan, per the reviewer's clarification). The picker offers "All year (ongoing)" or "From <month> on" (that month → December), so the user decides whether a mid-year commitment counts for the whole year or just the rest of it.
- `{type:'goalMonthly', goal, monthly}` — set a goal's monthly contribution. Moves its finish date and adjusts surplus + cash; **net worth unchanged** (the money stays yours).
- `{type:'income', amount, months?}` — a recurring income change (signed), with the same optional `months` scope as `expenseMonthly`.

Every user-facing "decision" (afford a purchase, accelerate a goal, plan a life event) is just a friendly entrance that constructs one of these — or, for a **life change**, a batch of them. The four life templates each compose a subset of `{expenseMonthly (new monthly cost), purchase (one-time upfront), income (a signed change)}`: **baby** (all three), **house** / **car** (monthly + upfront), **income change** (income only). Wren **states its assumption** in one line ("assumes the rest of the year tracks your plan"), because a projection is only as good as that assumption.

## 13. Monthly Nest Review

A deterministic recap of the **last completed month**, built by `buildNestReview()` — a pure read of already-computed state (`state.spend`/`state.dep` indexes, `state.nwHist`, goals, and the shared cash engine). No LLM; Wren narrates, never calculates (§11). It reviews the previous calendar month only when that month sits inside the live budget year's indexes (`bYear() === curYear()` and `curMonth() ≥ 2`); otherwise it returns `null` and nothing surfaces.

The review object carries five parts, each with a **"Why?"** drill-down (`nestReviewCards()` turns them into headline + `why[]` lines):

- **Spending** — actual vs plan for the month, per category (itemized bills folded into parents via `catSpend12`). Over-plan and under-plan categories are ranked; the top movers become the "Why?" lines.
- **Net worth** — the change between the latest snapshot at/before the review month and the latest strictly before it, split into asset movement and debt paydown (attribution per §10, from each snapshot's `detail`). Omitted when there aren't two snapshots to compare.
- **Goals** — contributions made that month (category-tagged goal movements land in the spend index under the goal name) plus pace; surfaces goals that got money or that are behind.
- **Income** — deposits that month vs the income plan.
- **Cash heads-up** — a *forward* look from `planMetrics(currentPlan())`: the tightest projected month and its margin against the Nest Egg Floor (§7a). The one non-retrospective card, because a review should also point ahead.

**Delivery.** A once-per-month, per-device surface: a dismissible bar (`maybeOfferReview()`, keyed in `localStorage` as `nw_review_<sheetId>` = the reviewed `YYYYMM`) offered only when there was real activity to review. It's also always reachable — a card on the Month tab and a Wren intent ("monthly review", "how did last month go") — both calling `openNestReview()`. Seeing or dismissing the bar records the month so it won't nag again.

## 14. Effortless: merchant normalization + auto-categorization

Cuts transaction-entry friction deterministically — no LLM, learning only from the user's own ledger.

- **`normalizeMerchant(raw)`** turns a noisy statement/receipt string ("SQ *BLUE BOTTLE 0123", "AMZN MKTP US*R8", "POS DEBIT WM SUPERCENTER #1234 TAMPA FL") into a clean canonical name ("Blue Bottle", "Amazon", "Walmart"). A `MERCHANT_BRANDS` table catches the heavy-abbreviation offenders first; otherwise it strips card-processor/bank prefixes, `* #` noise, trailing store/ref numbers, phone numbers, dates, and a trailing state code, then title-cases. Falls back to the original string if cleaning empties it.
- **`merchantKey(raw)`** = the normalized name reduced to `[a-z0-9]`. **`merchantCatIndex()`** builds `merchantKey → most-used category` from the ledger (deposits and goal movements excluded), so raw variants of one store vote together.
- **`suggestCatFor(raw, idx?)`** returns the learned category: exact key match, then a **prefix fallback** — when the import key and a learned key (both ≥5 chars) share a prefix, the longest match wins — so a statement's store tail ("bluebottlecoffeesf") still matches the learned "bluebottle". Unknown merchants return "" (never a guess).

**Where it applies.** (1) The **Add form**: typing a company auto-fills the category from history — exact `companyMap()` first, then `suggestCatFor` for normalized matches — with a "✨ Picked from your history" hint you can override. (2) **Receipt scan**: the merchant is normalized and, if the receipt named no category, one is suggested from history. (3) **Import**: `impAutoCategorize()` runs once per parsed batch — normalizes every merchant for display and fills any **blank** category from history (rows the file already categorized are left for the tidy step), marking auto-filled rows `_auto` (shown with a ✨ tag and counted in the review header). Editing an auto-filled row clears the flag. Learning is self-reinforcing: every categorized transaction that lands in the ledger teaches the index for next time.

## 11. Wren's contract (the whole point)

Wren is a **narrator, not a calculator.** She receives already-computed facts —
`spent_this_month`, `category_variance`, `annual_plan_surplus`, `net_worth_change`, `goal_pace`, historical averages — and turns them into conversation. She must **never** derive a dollar figure from raw transaction text herself. The deterministic engine produces the number; Wren produces the sentence. This is what keeps a financial app from inventing figures.

Layers she speaks to: **Ask** (navigation/how-to), **Analyze** (what happened / why), **Plan** (forecasts and what-ifs). All from the numbers above. Her forecasts consume the **shared** engines rather than her own copies: the year-end "at this pace" projection is `yearEndSpendProjection()` (active-month average, the same function the Insights card uses — never YTD ÷ fraction-of-calendar-year, which would over-project a mid-year adopter); her monthly totals come from `monthActualTotals`; her expense classification is `isExpenseRow` (via `_isExp`); "find money" reserves both remaining category budget **and** unfunded fixed-goal commitments before calling anything "uncommitted"; her cash-flow/sustainability answer reads `planMetrics(currentPlan())` (the same numbers the Cash Flow screen and Decision Engine show); and a "cut $X/month" what-if gets its freed-up annual amount from `evaluateDecision`, not an ad-hoc ×12. She narrates; the engine computes.
