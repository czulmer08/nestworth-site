About NestWorth

How NestWorth works — and how we test it.

NestWorth was built around one stubborn idea: your budget, your cash flow, your savings goals and your net worth should never tell four different stories. This page explains how the app is put together, and how carefully its math is checked — because when it comes to your money, "probably right" isn't good enough.

The idea

Most money apps are a pile of separate calculators bolted together — a budget here, a net-worth tracker there — that quietly disagree with each other. NestWorth does the math once, in a single financial model, and every screen reads from that same model. Your paycheck schedule, the money you set aside for goals, the cushion you keep for a rough month — they all flow through one set of calculations, so the numbers stay honest with each other.

It also runs entirely in your browser, on top of your own Google Drive. There's no NestWorth server holding your financial life. (The full details are in the privacy policy.)

How it works, briefly

When you open NestWorth, it creates a private budget spreadsheet in your Google Drive and does all the work from there — reading and writing that file directly from your phone. You log spending in a couple of taps; NestWorth turns it into a budget that understands real paydays, savings that count as savings, and a net-worth picture that updates as you go. Wren, the friendly bird, explains what's happening in plain English — and Wren reads the very same numbers the app computes everywhere else. NestWorth computes; Wren interprets.

How we test NestWorth

Because a budgeting app that's subtly wrong is worse than useless, the financial model is checked continuously, in several different ways. In plain terms:

  • One model, checked from every screen. Budget, cash flow, goals and net worth all read from the same underlying calculations, so they physically can't drift apart.
  • Known cases with known answers. 64 hand-built "golden" scenarios with a verified correct answer are re-checked on every single change.
  • Thousands of randomized households. We generate more than 9,000 complete, randomly-built households and confirm the rules that must always hold — for example: adding an expense can never improve your cash flow, and moving money into a goal never changes your net worth.
  • Wren tells the truth. Wren is checked against 122 real conversations to confirm it reports the exact figures the engine produced, and never invents its own.
  • A test of the tests. We deliberately break the math in known ways and confirm the tests catch every one — so a passing test actually means something.
  • Dropped connections and interrupted saves. We simulate the network failing at the worst possible moment and confirm NestWorth never silently loses a change, never claims a save it didn't make, and never duplicates a transaction.
  • Safety and speed. Automated checks for spreadsheet-formula injection, cross-site scripting and safe sharing, plus code-level accessibility checks and performance tested up to 100,000 transactions.

Altogether that's dozens of automated test suites running against the app — thousands of individual checks, most of them re-run on every change.

What this does — and doesn't — prove. These are automated tests in a controlled environment. They give us strong confidence that the math is correct and that the app recovers gracefully when things go wrong. They are not a substitute for testing on real phones with real people, which we also do — and no amount of testing makes software perfect. If you ever see a number that looks wrong, please tell us; we take it seriously.

A note on advice

NestWorth helps you see and plan your own money clearly. It doesn't provide financial, tax, legal or investment advice, and it isn't a substitute for a professional who knows your full situation.
